What Makes Customers Say Yes to a Digital Loyalty Program?

Survey analytics for a university farm store | AO4: Digital loyalty program adoption

Cecilia Espina

2026-10-27

The business problem

A university farm store is growing, but its data is not.

  • Loyalty is a paper punch card: no data on who comes back or why.
  • Promotions are not measured, so the store can’t tell what brings customers back.
  • Large grocers already offer apps and digital rewards.
  • Customer attitudes toward a digital program are unknown.

Note

Question for the business: Would customers actually use a digital loyalty program, and what would make them more willing?

Why it matters

The online channel is where growth is.

  • Online grocery is forecast to grow about 9% a year through 2029, vs. under 2% for in-store grocery (The Packer 2025).
  • A digital program is the clearest gap versus competitors.
  • It would replace the punch card with consented purchase histories the store can analyze.

So what: differentiation and convenience matter, so loyalty is one piece of a connected online system (discovery, conversion, assortment, loyalty).

Who this is for

Primary audience: the store’s manager and director (the people deciding whether to launch and how to message a digital program).

What past research says

  • Usefulness, ease of use, and trust predict whether customers adopt loyalty programs and apps (Donnelly et al. 2015; Ieva and Ziliani 2017; Lin et al. 2019; Löffler and Bodendorf 2010; Su et al. 2022).
  • Preferences differ by customer (Ieva and Ziliani 2017; Rahadian et al. 2026).
  • Little work covers small university stores.

Method direction: a survey, with regression of intention on usefulness, ease, and trust, plus intention by visit frequency as a preview of RFM segments.

Research question and hypothesis

RQ4: What drives customers’ intention to use a digital loyalty program at the store?

H4: Perceived usefulness, ease of use, and trust in data handling are each positively associated with intention to use.

Data and sampling

Source: primary data, an online survey built in Qualtrics.

  • Population: customers 18 or older who bought something in the past 12 months
  • Sampling: non-probability convenience sample
  • Target: n = 200
  • Recruitment: in-store flyer with QR code, plus email
  • Incentive: an extra punch on the customer’s loyalty card

Data preparation

Workflow in R (tidyverse) inside Quarto:

  1. Import the Qualtrics export
  2. Tidy: clean names, drop incomplete responses, recode, reverse-code the trust item
  3. Transform: build composite scores (mean of items per construct)
  4. Visualize and model
library(tidyverse)
library(psych)

survey <- read_csv("data/survey_clean.csv")   # [REPLACE with your export]

survey <- survey |>
  mutate(
    trust_3 = 6 - trust_3,                       # reverse-code (5-point scale)
    pu      = rowMeans(across(starts_with("pu_"))),
    peou    = rowMeans(across(starts_with("peou_"))),
    trust   = rowMeans(across(starts_with("trust_"))),
    intent  = rowMeans(across(starts_with("intent_"))),
    attitude = rowMeans(across(starts_with("att_")))
  )

Analysis plan

  1. Reliability and descriptives
  2. Multiple linear regression for H4
  3. Exploratory attitude model
  4. Compare segments on the RFM preview (ANOVA)
  5. Themes from open-ended answers
  6. Structural equation model as an alternative

Limitations

  • Non-probability sample: results may not generalize to all customers.
  • Stated intention, not enrollment: true RFM needs post-launch purchase data.
  • Survey shows association, not cause.
  • Timeline depended on IRB approval; a late start shortens the response window.

Next steps

  • Launch the program with a small pilot and track real enrollment.
  • Replace the RFM preview with true RFM once purchase data exists.
  • Re-survey after launch to compare intention vs. actual use.
